# INDIA-AI-001A — Evidence Log

## 2026-08-28 — Systemic-friction evidence review

### Finding 1 — Problem remains material despite multiple interventions
The Ministry of MSME Annual Report 2025-26 records 256,892 delayed-payment applications involving ₹55,244.31 crore through MSME Samadhaan up to 31 December 2025. It reports 52,744 applications still awaiting review, involving ₹8,397.25 crore, and 53,911 cases disposed by MSEFCs involving ₹14,638.38 crore. New delayed-payment cases have been filed on the MSME ODR Portal since 15 October 2025, with integration into Samadhaan.

### Finding 2 — Government has moved beyond filing/tracking toward structured dispute intervention
The official ODR guidelines define a pre-MSEFC stage with two voluntary processes: Digital Guided Pathway and Unmanned Negotiation. The legal MSEFC stage then includes conciliation/mediation and arbitration.

### Finding 3 — ODR shows both successful resolution and continuing system-level demand
A July 2026 Ministry/PIB release reports individual ODR cases resolved rapidly, including one ₹21.06 lakh delayed-payment claim settled within 24 hours, while also describing the ODR portal as a mechanism intended to reduce dependence on lengthy litigation and preserve commercial relationships.

### Finding 4 — Policy is being redesigned at the transaction/payment layer
In June 2026 the Government mandated TReDS routing for settlement of invoices from all operating Central Public Sector Enterprises to MSME suppliers. The stated objective is to improve payment discipline and unlock working capital.

## Interpretation

The evidence strengthens the hypothesis that delayed payment is not merely a missing-software problem. India is simultaneously applying filing/tracking, ODR, negotiation, mediation, arbitration and invoice-financing/payment-routing interventions. The persistence of large claim volumes indicates that the transaction/payment/dispute architecture itself should be investigated.

However, this does NOT prove that the Ministry has explicitly declared a need to redesign the broader culture or architecture of B2B transactions. That remains a discovery hypothesis and must be kept separate from the owner-declared AI negotiation challenge.
